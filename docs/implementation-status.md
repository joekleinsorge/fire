# Implementation status

Open FI Planner is now a usable local planning application rather than a landing page or single-input calculator. It provides a multi-screen workspace backed by a shared monthly household plan. This is a substantial working implementation, while the broader five-stage product specification remains a roadmap rather than a claim of completion.

## What works

The browser workspace covers a six-input quick FI calculator with linked savings edits, a savings-rate comparison chart and expansion into a full plan, plus an overview, editable assets and debts, recurring income and spending, retirement assumptions and simulations, scenario comparison, progress observations, account settings, and child planning. Child profiles can hold separate 529 and Trump Account forecasts, including education-use assumptions and Trump Account contributions through the year the child turns 17. Child account balances never enter the parent's investable assets or FI target, but household-funded contributions (and, when enabled, education shortfalls the household covers) are paid from household cash in the account withdrawal order and reduce the parent's projection; unaffordable amounts are reported as unfunded. Employer, pilot, and contributions marked external do not affect household cash. Future purchases model one-time costs plus ongoing or time-limited upkeep in today's dollars. Open-ended upkeep raises the FI target as perpetual spending. Every cost that ends and falls after retirement starts (debt and purchase loan payments, one-time purchases net of sale proceeds, upkeep with a term or a later sale, and household child funding) is added to the target as retirement obligations, a present value discounted at the plan return. A purchase can be partly financed (a fixed-payment loan at its quoted rate whose payments start the next month and shrink in today's dollars with an expected inflation rate), kept as a non-spendable asset with an assumed growth rate, and can sell an existing account or an asset bought by an earlier purchase (less selling costs, repaying that purchase's own loan and stopping its upkeep) and pay off a debt from the proceeds, which covers moving to a new home and moving again. Each asset can be sold once, and disabled purchases never fail on stale account or debt references. The purchases table shows each purchase's effect on FI age and spendable assets at retirement, and a removal can be undone. Debts are entered at their quoted rate with an optional expected inflation rate; fixed payments (calculated, or entered) then shrink in today's dollars and interest accrues at the real rate. Unpaid debt is capitalized and each shortfall is reported once, including after a loan's maturity. Child account year-end events fall in the December after a month-end valuation (a December valuation starts with the next year). A purchase scenario saves a full independent copy of the plan with its starting baseline so the two paths can be compared and either can be opened. Projections report both the FI age at or after planned retirement and the earliest age spendable assets reach the target, so trade-offs that move the crossing remain visible when retirement age is fixed. Signed-in users reopen their last plan on load, and scenarios or observations created before signing in are saved with the plan's first save. Plans can be calculated anonymously in a working session. Local accounts can save versioned plans; Settings lists saved revisions and restores older inputs as a new version. Plans also support scenario records, comparison, and progress observations, including confirmed observation removal. JSON import/export and CSV exports are available.

The Python standard-library server uses SQLite for transactional persistence and a durable local job queue. It runs as a single server/worker process. Household isolation and plan roles are enforced at the API. This is a deliberate initial deployment choice instead of the target PostgreSQL/distributed-worker topology.

The monthly engine models real-dollar balances, scheduled after-tax streams, account-specific real returns and fees, availability and withdrawal order, a first-phase tax treatment per account (after-tax, pre-tax, Roth, or taxable, each with one flat withdrawal tax rate that grosses up withdrawals funding spending), debt payments, essential and desired spending outcomes, and FI target timing based on modeled retirement cash flows. Seeded simulation produces outcome denominators and annual percentile bands. Property and other assets count toward net worth but are unavailable for spending by default, with fixed assumed returns and no automatic sale or linked debt.

## Boundaries

The quick estimator assumes annual savings arrive at each projection-year end. It does not simulate retirement spending. The full-plan progress ratio uses accounts available for spending; observations map to monthly periods after the recorded valuation baseline. Scenario asset paths align on common ages only when currency and price basis match.

The calculations are planning estimates, not a ledger, tax filing, benefits determination, or guarantee. The model uses floating-point arithmetic and simplified monthly debt interest. Stream values are entered after tax. Account withdrawals pay a flat, user-entered effective rate per account (defaults: pre-tax 22%, taxable 10%, Roth and after-tax 0%), reported as `withdrawal_tax`; deposits, purchase sales, and growth are untaxed. The FI target is the after-tax target (`after_tax_target`) grossed up by `target_tax_rate`, the balance-weighted withdrawal rate of available accounts at retirement start, so it is compared with pre-tax account balances. The selected currency controls display formatting; there is no exchange-rate conversion. Users must supply return assumptions, income, expenses, benefits, healthcare costs, and other inputs they want represented.

The app does not provide statutory federal/state/international taxes (no brackets, capital-gains basis, or tax on contributions or growth; the flat per-account rate is an assumption, not a tax calculation), Social Security estimates, ACA/Medicare eligibility, RMD/Roth/SEPP rules, reviewed named withdrawal strategies, mortality or joint-life transitions, jurisdiction-specific estate calculations, complete property transaction accounting, or professional multi-client workflows. Child account forecasts do not validate 529 contribution/program limits, qualified education expenses, state tax treatment, Trump Account eligibility or tax treatment, pilot eligibility, withdrawals, or post-18 IRA rules. Only the Trump Account contribution caps are checked: the statutory 2026–27 amounts, with later inflation-indexed caps held at the same amount in today's dollars. These are not a tax module. Trump Account contributions stop by the projected growth-period end year, while any later balance continues under the user's assumed return and fees. These areas need reviewed source modules and remain release gates.
